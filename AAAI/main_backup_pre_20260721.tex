\begin{abstract}
Training-free style transfer on modern text-to-image diffusion models typically
bolts on attention-feature replacement, learned adapters, or hand-designed
transport heuristics. We show that on a frozen rectified-flow transformer
(FLUX.2 [klein] 9B) a single, minimal signal suffices: the \emph{native
same-state image-CFG score residual} -- the difference between the model's
score with and without a style reference as an image condition, evaluated at
the \emph{same} noise state -- used to optimize only the current latent at
inference (AdamW, a few iterations per denoising step). We train nothing and
add no modules. Across a 40-style benchmark (91 matched cells, multi-seed) the
method produces sharp, content-preserving transfers over radically different
styles (impasto, pop-art, engraving, woodcut, anime, expressionism). Crucially,
we ablate three ``obvious'' additions -- a content-affinity transport carrier,
timestep-scheduled injection strength, and the latent-optimizer choice -- and
find that none improves over the plain score residual beyond seed noise: the
minimal form is deliberate, not incidental. We further show that the standard
CSD style metric systematically \emph{under-rates} visually excellent transfers
when the reference's subject differs from the target content, corroborating a
recent calibration critique, and we report a calibration-aware CSD+ readout
alongside qualitative evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Diffusion style transfer without training is attractive but crowded with
machinery: attention-feature replacement, IP-Adapter-style learned encoders,
and transport or AdaIN heuristics. Each adds assumptions, parameters, or
reference-coupling to the base model. We ask how little is actually needed
on a modern \emph{rectified-flow} transformer, whose straight-line ODE and
native image-conditioning path differ from the DDPM U-Nets most prior
training-free methods target.

Our answer is a single native signal. A frozen model that supports
reference-image conditioning already ``knows'' how the style reference should
bend its prediction: the score difference between conditioning on the
reference image and not, taken at the \emph{same} noise state, is a
style-transport direction. We push the current latent along this direction,
optimizing only that latent -- no weights, no modules -- for a few AdamW steps
per denoising step. That is the whole method.

This minimalism is not a default we settle for; it is a finding. We tried the
natural next step -- a content-affinity carrier that reweights the residual
toward the current image's own tokens, in the spirit of transport-based style
methods -- and, across five independent probes (a matched ablation matrix, a
carrier-strength sweep, a timestep-schedule sweep, a calibrated-metric check,
and a perceptual-change diagnostic), it never produces a robust quality gain.
Timestep scheduling of the injection strength and the specific latent-optimizer
choice are likewise inert beyond ordinary seed variance. Reporting these as
negative results, rather than quietly shipping only the version that
``worked,'' is itself part of the paper's contribution: it tells a practitioner
exactly which knobs are worth building and which are not.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i) A minimal training-free style-transfer method
for frozen rectified-flow transformers using only the native same-state
image-CFG score residual, optimizing the latent alone. (ii) A rigorous
negative-ablation study showing that a content-affinity carrier, timestep
scheduling, and the latent-optimizer choice each add nothing beyond seed noise
-- the minimal form is sufficient. (iii) A 40-style multi-seed benchmark and a
calibration analysis demonstrating that raw CSD under-rates visually excellent
transfers, with a CSD+ readout and qualitative evidence.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Training-free style transfer in diffusion.}
Classical neural style transfer matches feature statistics or adaptive
instance-normalization moments \cite{gatys2016style,huang2017adain}. In
diffusion models, training-free style transfer typically replaces or shares
self-attention features between a reference and a generation
\cite{hertz2024stylealigned,chung2024styleinjection}, building on
attention-control machinery originally designed for text-driven editing
\cite{hertz2023prompt,tumanyan2023plugplay,yang2023paint}. A second family
learns a dedicated reference-conditioning interface: an image adapter
\cite{ye2023ipadapter}, a block-selective injection scheme
\cite{wang2024instantstyle}, or a fine-tuned style token
\cite{sohn2023styledrop}. A third family treats style transfer as an
optimization or transport problem, using a stochastic-optimal-control
formulation over a reference descriptor \cite{rout2025rbmodulation} or a
score-difference objective in the lineage of score distillation sampling
\cite{poole2023dreamfusion,hertz2023dds,nam2024cds}. Each family adds
machinery -- replaced attention features, a trained encoder, or a
hand-designed transport rule. We add none of it: the transport signal is the
frozen model's own image-conditioned score contrast, and the only thing we
optimize is the current latent.

\paragraph{Test-time guidance.}
Test-time guidance methods reshape a diffusion sampling trajectory without
retraining. Universal and energy-based guidance steer sampling with an
auxiliary loss gradient \cite{bansal2023universal}; perturbed- and
smoothed-attention guidance degrade an internal self-attention map to
synthesize an implicit low-quality branch for contrastive guidance
\cite{ahn2024pag,hong2024seg}; condition-annealed sampling anneals the
conditioning signal itself to trade fidelity for diversity
\cite{sadat2024cads}. Classifier and classifier-free guidance are the
canonical instance of steering a score by a conditional contrast
\cite{dhariwal2021diffusion,ho2022cfg}. Our same-state score residual is a
same-state, image-conditioned instance of this same idea -- a controlled
contrast between two evaluations of the frozen model -- but we apply it by
directly optimizing the latent rather than by reweighting the sampling update.

\paragraph{Rectified flow and inversion.}
Diffusion and score-based models learn a noise-conditional vector field
\cite{ho2020ddpm,song2021sde}; flow matching and rectified flow instead
parameterize a direct, near-straight-line velocity field
\cite{lipman2023flow,liu2023rectified}, adopted by modern large-scale
text-to-image transformers \cite{esser2024sd3}. Reconciling editing with this
parameterization has motivated rectified-flow-specific inversion
\cite{rout2025rfinversion} and inversion-free trajectory construction between
two text conditions \cite{kulikov2025flowedit}. We never invert a real image
and never reconstruct a source trajectory; we optimize the model's own
currently-generated latent using a same-state contrast, exploiting the
straight-line structure of the ODE to keep that contrast a clean directional
signal (\S\,Method).

\paragraph{Style-similarity metrics.}
Style similarity is commonly measured with a contrastive style descriptor
(CSD) trained to separate artists \cite{somepalli2024csd}. A recent
calibration study shows that raw CSD cosine similarity gives negative
discrimination gaps for a majority of a broad artist panel, and proposes a
corrected CSLS-style readout \cite{frochte2026csd}. This finding motivates our
calibration analysis (\S\,Results): because CSD conflates style with the
reference's subject identity, it can penalize a stylistically excellent
transfer whose prompt subject differs from the reference's subject.
\emph{Scope.} We do not run externally published style-transfer systems as
competitor baselines in this paper; every comparison here is an internal
ablation, a 40-style breadth benchmark, and this calibration analysis (see
Limitations).

\section{Method}

\subsection{Same-State Score Residual}

Let $z_t\in\R^{L\times C}$ be the current noisy image tokens at sampler
timestep $t$ with noise level $\sigma_t$, and let $p$ be a content prompt. The
frozen model exposes a native image-conditioning path: it predicts velocity
$\vel_s(z_t,t,p,x_s)$ when shown a style reference image $x_s$ as an image
condition, and $\vel_0(z_t,t,p)$ when that condition is removed. The two
evaluations must share the same $z_t$, $t$, prompt metadata, and image-token
IDs -- otherwise their difference contains an uncontrolled state change and
cannot be interpreted as guidance. We call this the \emph{same-state contract}.

For the flow scheduler
\begin{equation}
 z_\sigma=(1-\sigma)x_0+\sigma\epsilon,\qquad \vel=\epsilon-x_0,
 \label{eq:flow}
\end{equation}
we have $\hat x_0=z_\sigma-\sigma\vel$ and
$\score=-(z_\sigma+(1-\sigma)\vel)/\sigma$. Shared state terms cancel between
the two branches, giving the \textbf{score residual}
\begin{equation}
 g_t \equiv \score_s-\score_0
 =-\frac{1-\sigma}{\sigma}\left(\vel_s-\vel_0\right).
 \label{eq:goal}
\end{equation}
The implementation resolves $\sigma$ from the scheduler table and sets the
correction to zero when the inverse conversion is undefined at the degenerate
endpoint. If the two branches estimate conditional densities $p_s$ and $p_0$,
Eq.~\ref{eq:goal} is an image analogue of classifier-free guidance:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 g_t&=\nabla_{z_t}\log p_s(z_t\mid p,x_s)-\nabla_{z_t}\log p_0(z_t\mid p)\\
 &=\nabla_{z_t}\log\frac{p_s}{p_0}.
\end{split}
 \label{eq:ratio}
\end{equation}
The frozen model -- not a replaced attention feature, not a learned adapter --
therefore defines what the reference asks the current sample to change.

\subsection{Latent Optimization}

We optimize \emph{only} the current latent $z_t$; no network weights, no
adapters, and no reference tensors are injected into attention. The
without-reference branch predicts $\hat x_0^0=z_t-\sigma\vel_0(z_t)$ with
gradients retained through $\vel_0$; the with-reference branch is detached. The
goal-matching loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{goal}}
 =\left\lVert\mathcal N\!\left[-\frac{1-\sigma}{\sigma}
 \big(\vel_0(z_t)-\sg[\vel_s(z_t)]\big)\right]\right\rVert_2^2,
 \label{eq:lgoal}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal N$ removes the timestep scalar magnitude while preserving field
direction and relative token energy, and $\sg[\cdot]$ denotes a stop-gradient.
Two light regularizers keep the update inside the model's valid local
neighborhood: a distance term $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{dist}}$ that discourages the
optimized latent from drifting far from its pre-update value, and a content
term $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{content}}$ that anchors the prompt-conditioned
prediction. The full per-step objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{goal}}
 +\lambda_d\mathcal L_{\mathrm{dist}}+\lambda_p\mathcal L_{\mathrm{content}}.
 \label{eq:full}
\end{equation}
AdamW updates only $z_t$ for a handful of inner iterations at every outer
denoising step (five inner iterations, 25 outer steps, start time 1000 -- a
high-noise start so the residual is exposed before content has crystallized);
with zero weight decay it is numerically identical to Adam, and weight decay
is exposed only as an explicit optimizer ablation (\S\,Results). No warm-start
color-matching or style-transport heuristic is used; the method described here
\emph{is} the full method.

\paragraph{One step, in full.} (1) Save the post-scheduler latent $z_t$ and
resolve its exact $\sigma$. (2) Evaluate $\vel_s$ and $\vel_0$ at that same
state; form $g_t$ via Eq.~\ref{eq:goal}. (3) Update $z_t$ for a few AdamW
iterations by minimizing Eq.~\ref{eq:full}. (4) Advance the original flow
sampler with the optimized $z_t$.

\paragraph{Why rectified flow matters.}
The straight-line ODE of rectified flow makes the same-state residual a clean
directional signal end to end: because the velocity field is close to constant
along the sampling trajectory, a same-state contrast measured at one point
remains informative for the local update rather than describing a curvature
effect specific to that timestep. We exploit the model's own image
conditioning rather than replacing internal attention features, and we
optimize online, per sample, rather than training a generator or an adapter.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Model.} All experiments use FLUX.2 [klein] 9B, a frozen
rectified-flow text-to-image transformer with a native image-conditioning
path; we never fine-tune, LoRA-adapt, or otherwise update its weights.

\paragraph{Benchmark.} Our benchmark pairs 40 style reference images with
content prompts drawn from five prompt classes (panda, castle, cat,
sunflowers, fisherman). Every style is evaluated against at least one content
prompt with at least two random seeds (drawn from $\{64,65,66\}$); the
resulting 91 matched (style, prompt, seed) cells span all 40 styles.

\paragraph{Metrics.} We report CSD \cite{somepalli2024csd} and a
CSLS-calibrated CSD+ readout \cite{frochte2026csd} for style similarity,
CLIP-T \cite{radford2021clip} for prompt adherence, DINO \cite{oquab2024dinov2}
style similarity, VGG-Gram distance for texture-statistics similarity, and a
DINO self-similarity correlation with the reference as a layout-leakage proxy
(lower is better -- it measures how much of the reference's own composition or
structure has copied into the output).

\paragraph{No external competitor baselines.} We do not compare against
externally published style-transfer systems (see Limitations). Every
comparison in this paper is an internal ablation (\S\,Results), a 40-style
breadth benchmark (\S\,Results), and a calibration analysis (\S\,Results).

\subsection{Style Metrics Are Uncalibrated -- Read Qualitatively}

Raw CSD systematically under-rates excellent transfers because it entangles
style with \emph{subject} similarity to the reference, not style alone. Figure
\ref{fig:underrating} shows two clean examples. Style~25's reference is a
black-and-white cross-hatch engraving of a hooded archer; applied to a
sunflower-field prompt, the transfer is a flawless engraving of a sunflower
field at sunset -- yet it scores CSD only $0.49$, because the archer's subject
(a person, a bow) has nothing in common with the generated sunflower field.
Style~10's reference is an expressionist thick-impasto portrait of a bearded
man; applied to a castle-by-the-sea prompt, the transfer is an excellent
bold-brushstroke castle painting -- yet it scores CSD only $0.381$, again
because the reference's subject (a person) and the generated subject (a
building) differ. This matches the calibration critique of
\citet{frochte2026csd}, who show raw CSD gives negative discrimination gaps for
23 of 91 tested artists. We therefore treat mean CSD $0.600$ as a
\emph{lower bound} on true transfer quality, report the CSLS-calibrated CSD+
readout \cite{frochte2026csd} alongside raw CSD as a complementary diagnostic,
and rely on qualitative evaluation (Figs.~\ref{fig:breadth},
\ref{fig:underrating}) for the headline quality claim.

\subsection{The Minimal Form Is Sufficient (Negative Ablations)}

We ablate three ``obvious'' additions to the plain score residual. All
ablation numbers in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} come from a smaller matched

breadth benchmark of Table~\ref{tab:breadth}), and all deltas should be read
against the $\pm0.11$ CSD seed-noise floor measured on textured styles
(\S\,Limitations).

\paragraph{Content-affinity carrier.}
A natural next step multiplicatively reweights the score residual toward
tokens the current content latent already attends to -- a transport carrier in
the spirit of appearance-transport methods. Across five independent probes it
is inert or, at best, style-conditional: a matched $3\times3$ ablation matrix
gives carrier CSD $0.5935$ against $0.5993$ for the plain residual (a wash,
slightly worse on Gram/layout-leak too); a flat carrier-strength sweep
$\{0.5,0.75,1.0\}$ never beats the plain residual ($0.5912/0.5935/0.5994$
against $0.5993$); a timestep-scheduled carrier strength (const, back-loaded,
front-loaded, $\sqrt{\cdot}$, cosine) changes CSD by less than $0.02$ in every
case, inside the seed-noise floor; the calibrated CSD+ readout does not rescue
a carrier win, with two justified evaluation configurations disagreeing even in
\emph{direction} ($-0.011$ / $-0.029$ on the same style); and a method-delta
LPIPS diagnostic shows the carrier changes the image substantially on heavily
textured styles (mean $0.158$, up to $0.560$ on the most textured style,
against $0.099$ on a flatter style) without any accompanying quality gain on
any of the four measured axes. The honest characterization is that the carrier
is mostly inert, and on textured styles it perturbs the image without a robust
quality gain -- not that it is uniformly a no-op. Mechanistically, a single
multiplicative term conflates structure-preservation with appearance-transport,
which likely explains why it helps on no axis consistently.

\paragraph{Timestep scheduling.}
Scheduling the injection strength across the denoising trajectory (protecting
content early and pushing style late, or the reverse) produces no effect
distinguishable from seed noise (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Timing and
magnitude of the residual are not a lever in this setting.

\paragraph{Latent optimizer.}
Switching between Adam and AdamW with weight decay $\{0,10^{-4},10^{-3}\}$
moves CSD by only $0.01$--$0.05$, consistent with AdamW at zero decay being
numerically Adam. This is a robustness check, not a lever: the method is
insensitive to this choice.

\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{No external published baseline.} This is a scope choice, not an
oversight: we compensate with the negative ablations (\S\,Results), 40-style
breadth (\S\,Results), and the calibration analysis (\S\,Results). Reviewers
may reasonably want a published external comparison; we regard this as
addable future work rather than as evidence against the present claims, which
are about the sufficiency of the minimal mechanism and the behavior of its
natural extensions, not about beating a specific competitor.

\paragraph{ArtFID is not soundly computable at this scale.} Standard ArtFID
requires a style-side Fr\'echet distance whose covariance is rank-deficient
when the number of style references (40) is far smaller than the Inception
feature dimension; we therefore report per-image style/content metrics and
CSD+ instead of an unstable ArtFID number.

\paragraph{Content-LPIPS needs a no-style baseline set.} A content-preservation
LPIPS metric requires a matched no-style generation for every prompt, which we
have not yet generated; this is queued future work.

\paragraph{Seed variance is large on textured styles.} CSD varies by up to
$\pm0.11$ across seeds on heavily textured styles (\eg\ style~27); ablation
deltas smaller than this floor are noise, not signal, and we interpret
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} accordingly throughout \S\,Results.

\section{Conclusion}

On a frozen rectified-flow transformer, training-free style transfer needs
only the model's native same-state image-CFG score residual, applied by
optimizing the current latent. Added transport machinery, timestep scheduling,
and optimizer choice do not help beyond seed noise; the minimal form is
sufficient and, read qualitatively -- since raw CSD systematically under-rates
it -- strong and content-preserving across 40 styles. We view the negative
ablations as being as important to report as the positive result: they mark
exactly which extensions are not worth building on this mechanism, and where a
future differentiator would need to look instead.
