Scope: survey strongest 2023-2026 top-venue training-free style-transfer mechanisms
and bind each to a concrete, borrowable recipe for our method (FLUX.2 [klein] 9B,
frozen, latent-only test-time optimization, native same-state image-CFG score
residual as the style "goal", carrier/content-affinity reweighting empirically dead).

project pages, or CVF openaccess listings fetched during this session; where a
paper's full-text methodology could not be independently confirmed (paywalled PDF

## 1. Paper table

| Paper | Venue / Year | 1-line mechanism | Where/how style is injected |
|---|---|---|---|
| **StyleAligned** ("Style Aligned Image Generation via Shared Attention", Hertz et al.) | CVPR 2024, arXiv:2312.02133 | Batch of images shares self-attention so a reference's style "leaks" into targets with minimal disruption | Target query/key are AdaIN-normalized toward the reference's query/key stats (`Q̂_t=AdaIN(Q_t,Q_r)`, `K̂_t=AdaIN(K_t,K_r)`), then target attends over concatenated `[K_r;K_t]`,`[V_r;V_t]`. Applied across ~70 self-attn layers of SDXL U-Net. Single-reference case uses DDIM inversion of the reference to get its trajectory. |
| **StyleID** ("Style Injection in Diffusion", Chung et al.) | CVPR 2024 Highlight, arXiv:2312.09008 | Direct K/V substitution from style image into content self-attention, with 3 stabilizers | K,V of content self-attn (decoder layers 7–12 of SD) replaced by style K,V at matched timestep; **query preservation** `Q̃=γQ_content+(1-γ)Q_style` (γ=0.75 default); **attention temperature scaling** softmax(τ·QKᵀ/√d), τ=1.5; **initial-latent AdaIN** `z_T^cs = σ(z_T^s)(z_T^c-μ(z_T^c))/σ(z_T^c)+μ(z_T^s)` for color harmony. | **Visual Style Prompting (VSP)** ("Visual Style Prompting with Swapping Self-Attention", Jeong et al.) | arXiv:2402.12974, 2024 | Style transfer purely via *late-layer, no-blend* K/V swap — deliberately skips AdaIN | Full substitution `Attn(Qˡ,K_rˡ,V_rˡ)`, **no blending, no AdaIN**, applied only from the 24th SDXL layer onward (late up-blocks only); down-blocks/bottleneck left untouched. Content leakage is controlled purely by *layer selection*, all T=50 DDIM steps. |
| **Z-STAR / Z\*** ("Zero-shot Style Transfer via Attention Rearrangement", Deng et al.) | CVPR 2024, arXiv:2311.16491 | Joint-softmax attention rearrangement over two independently-inverted (content, style) DDIM paths | Concatenate content-vs-style and content-vs-content similarity logits *before* softmax: `A' = softmax([λ·Q_c K_sᵀ/√d, Q_c K_cᵀ/√d])`, λ=1.2. Applied at U-Net decoder layers 10–15, timesteps 5→30 of a 30-step schedule. |
| **RB-Modulation** ("Training-Free Personalization via Stochastic Optimal Control", Rout et al.) | ICLR 2025, arXiv:2405.17401 | Recasts style personalization as stochastic optimal control: a *terminal cost* on a style descriptor drives a control term added to the reverse SDE drift | Terminal cost `h(x̂_0)=‖Ψ(x_0^ref)-Ψ(E[x_0|x_t])‖²` (Ψ = style descriptor, x̂_0 via Tweedie's formula). Content leakage further suppressed by subtracting the content-text embedding from the image embedding before cross-attention injection. |
| **Scheduled Style Injection** (Kulkarni et al.) | CVPR-W (NTIRE) 2026, arXiv:2605.26538 | Shows StyleID's single global blend constant is provably suboptimal; replaces it with a timestep/layer schedule | Same `Q̃=γQ_c+(1-γ)Q_cs` as StyleID, but γ is now a function of normalized position `α=i/(N-1)` via warping `f(α)∈{α,α²,√α,(1-cos(απ))/2,(eᵅ-1)/(e-1)}`; best schedules (cosine, √α) **decrease** γ from 0.75→0.375 over the 50 DDIM steps ("high content-preservation early, strong style late"), giving ArtFID 26.98–27.0 vs. StyleID's 28.8 (~6% better) with zero extra params. |
| **Untwisting RoPE** ("Frequency Control for Shared Attention in DiTs", Mikaeili et al.) | arXiv:2602.05013, 2026 (FLUX-family DiT) | Diagnoses *why* DiT shared-attention style transfer over-copies reference layout: RoPE's high-frequency bands are strongly position-local and dominate attention, forcing queries toward the spatially-aligned reference token instead of a semantically similar one | Frequency-dependent rescaling of RoPE angles on the reference tokens: `s_d = s_hf + (s_lf - s_hf)·d̃^β`, `d̃=d/(D/2-1)`, with `s_hf<1` attenuating high-frequency (positional/local) components and `s_lf>1` amplifying low-frequency (semantic/global) ones. Two knobs (`s_hf`,`s_lf`) give a continuous copy-vs-style-only dial; demonstrated on Flux-family DiTs; training-free, inference-time only. Exact block-type (single- vs double-stream) not confirmed from the pages fetched. |
| **Training-Free High-Fidelity Appearance Transfer via DiTs** (Gu et al.) | ICASSP 2026, arXiv:2603.26767 | FLUX-specific (FLUX.1-Depth) K/V-context-expansion attention sharing, gated by a rectified-flow inversion trajectory and geometric (depth/mask) priors | Source query kept unchanged; K/V context is *expanded*, not swapped: `K'_src=[K_src;K̃_ref]`, `V'_src=[V_src;Ṽ_ref]`. High-fidelity RF inversion (FireFlow-based solver) gives a source noise trajectory that is replayed for early/late timesteps (`i≥k`) and blended with reference guidance for `i<k`. Applied on "first two and last two layers" of *both* FLUX single-stream and double-stream blocks — i.e. a small, targeted subset of DiT blocks, not the whole network. |
| **Attention Distillation** ("A Unified Approach to Visual Characteristics Transfer", Zhou et al.) | CVPR 2025, arXiv:2502.20235 | Structurally closest analog to our own pipeline: a self-attention-based loss between a reference-denoising branch and the target branch is backpropagated **into the latent** at each step as an improved classifier guidance, rather than injected as fixed K/V substitution | Reported (could not independently verify exact loss equation past the abstract/project-page level — CVF HTML page 403'd, PDF exceeded fetch size limit, HTML mirror 404'd): an "attention distillation loss" measured between self-attention maps of an "ideal" vs "current" stylization is minimized by gradient descent directly on the noisy latent, combined with a separate content-preserving self-attention loss, and folded into the denoising loop as a guidance term (i.e., loss → gradient → added to the score/latent update each step, not injected as attention K/V override). ## 2. Borrowable ideas

### Idea A — Timestep/layer-scheduled injection strength (from Scheduled Style Injection + StyleID)

**(a) Mechanism.** StyleID uses one global scalar γ to blend content/style queries at every timestep and every one of its 6 target layers. Scheduled Style Injection (2026) shows this is provably suboptimal: sweeping γ as a function of denoising progress (cosine or √-shaped, high γ/content-preservation early → low γ/style-dominant late) beats the best fixed γ by ~6% ArtFID, with zero added compute or parameters.

**(b) How it plugs into our method.** Our carrier applies a single fixed strength (`--carrier-strength 1.0`, `--carrier-temperature 0.15`) uniformly across all 25 denoise steps / 5 inner AdamW iters — structurally identical to StyleID's single global γ, and it was found empirically inert at *any* strength. Instead of scaling the *whole* goal residual by one constant, scale the **push magnitude of `goal_score` into the latent update by a cosine/√ schedule over the 25 denoise steps** (weak push early while global layout is being laid down in rectified-flow's high-timestep regime, strong push late when texture/style details are resolved). Concretely: replace the single `carrier-strength` scalar with `strength(t) = strength_end + (strength_start - strength_end)·f(t/T)` for `f∈{cosine, sqrt}`, applied directly to how much of `raw_goal_theta` (or its AdamW gradient contribution) is added at each of the 5 inner iters per step. No new forward passes, no new losses — pure re-parameterization of an existing knob.

**(c) Expected effect.** Should reduce layout leakage at early/high-noise steps (where content structure is most fragile) while allowing full style strength once structure has stabilized — i.e., move along the style-fidelity/leakage Pareto frontier rather than sitting on a single point, which is exactly the failure mode our carrier sweep exposed (same point at every strength = no Pareto movement).

**(d) Risk.** This is a *reweighting of the same signal we already have*, only along the timestep axis instead of the "spatial content-affinity" axis our dead carrier used. If the goal residual itself carries no separable style-vs-layout information at any single timestep (i.e., the problem is not "when" but "what"), a timestep schedule may prove just as inert as our spatial carrier — this needs a quick ablation to distinguish before over-investing.

### Idea B — Tweedie-projected differentiable style-descriptor loss stacked on the native residual (from RB-Modulation's terminal cost)

**(a) Mechanism.** RB-Modulation reframes reference-style personalization as stochastic optimal control: it defines a terminal cost `h(x̂_0)=‖Ψ(x_ref)-Ψ(E[x_0|x_t])‖²` on a style descriptor Ψ evaluated at the Tweedie-estimated clean image, then does gradient descent on this cost each step and adds the resulting control term directly to the latent before the normal denoising step (`x̂_t = x_t + u`, `u ← u - η∇_u h`).

**(b) How it plugs into our method.** We already run test-time AdamW optimization of the latent — this is the *same* computational shape. Concretely: at each of our 25 denoise steps, in addition to the existing native score-residual push, compute the one-step Tweedie/x0-estimate from the current `z_t` (standard in rectified flow via the predicted velocity), run it (and the reference image) through **CSD** — a metric we already compute for evaluation — to get a differentiable style-similarity loss, backprop that loss to `z_t`, and add its gradient (scaled by a weight λ) into the same AdamW step that currently only follows `goal_score`. This is a genuinely *independent* signal (an explicit style-embedding-matching objective) rather than a reweighting of the existing residual, which is the structural reason our carrier (a pure reweighting/transport of the same signal) went empirically inert.

**(c) Expected effect.** Likely raises CSD/DINO style-sim directly (it's literally the optimization target), at some risk of pushing CLIP-T down or increasing layout leakage if λ is too high — the classic style-fidelity/content-preservation trade axis, but now via a genuinely separate gradient rather than a dead reweighting knob.

**(d) Risk.** Needs a working differentiable Tweedie/x0-estimate path in our rectified-flow sampler (should exist already, since flow matching gives a clean-image estimate from the predicted velocity at (z_t,t)), and CSD's backbone must be run in a differentiable (non-`no_grad`) mode — likely fine since it's a frozen ViT, but adds a second full forward pass (CSD encoder) per inner iter, i.e. real compute cost. Also risks "reward hacking" CSD in ways that don't generalize to DINO-style-sim/VGG-Gram if the two metrics disagree.

### Idea C — Source-side RoPE frequency decoupling on reference-conditioning tokens (from Untwisting RoPE)

**(a) Mechanism.** Untwisting RoPE shows that in DiT shared-attention style transfer, high-frequency RoPE components are strongly position-local and dominate attention scores, so the model ends up "copying" the reference at matching spatial positions instead of transferring only its semantic/style content; attenuating high-frequency RoPE bands on the reference's key tokens (while amplifying low-frequency/global bands) lets attention become similarity-driven rather than position-driven, giving a continuous style-vs-copy dial with two scalars.

**(b) How it plugs into our method.** Our "goal" is computed as a same-state score *difference*: score(with reference as image condition) − score(without). If FLUX.2's own internal attention (however it ingests the image condition) uses RoPE-style positional encoding on the reference tokens, then some of the "with-reference" forward pass's layout-copying behavior is *baked into the residual before we ever touch the carrier*. This idea proposes patching the frozen model's forward pass — specifically the RoPE angle computation applied to reference-image tokens during the "with-reference" score evaluation only — to attenuate high-frequency bands, producing a **cleaner goal residual at the source**, rather than trying to fix layout leakage after the fact via an external carrier (which is what failed).

**(c) Expected effect.** If FLUX.2's layout leakage originates in exactly this positional-locality mechanism, this could meaningfully move the CSD/DINO-style-sim vs. DINO-layout-leakage trade in the *residual itself*, upstream of anything the carrier or latent optimizer does — a structurally different lever than anything already tried.

**(d) Risk — the largest of the four ideas.** (1) It requires understanding and monkey-patching FLUX.2 [klein]'s internal image-conditioning attention/RoPE implementation, which is a nontrivial reverse-engineering task on a 9B DiT we don't have public methodology detail for; (2) it arguably stretches the "optimize latent ONLY" hard constraint — no weights are trained and nothing is learned, but it does touch model internals during the forward pass rather than being a pure post-hoc latent operation, so it needs an explicit call on whether this is in-scope before investing engineering time; (3) unconfirmed on FLUX.2 specifically — Untwisting RoPE's page did not confirm single- vs double-stream block targeting, and FLUX.2's image-conditioning path may not resemble the shared-attention setup the paper analyzes at all.

### Idea D — Content self-similarity as a *regularizing loss*, not a *transport-reweighting matrix* (from Splicing ViT Features / Plug-and-Play)

**(a) Mechanism.** Splicing ViT Features (CVPR 2022) and Plug-and-Play Diffusion Features (CVPR 2023) both use self-similarity of internal features (ViT key self-similarity; diffusion self-attention/spatial features) as a **structure descriptor** that is matched between source and output via an explicit *loss term* (or direct feature copy at the same spatial location), not used to reweight some other unrelated signal.

**(b) How it plugs into our method.** Our dead carrier used content self-affinity as a **transport reweighting kernel** applied to the goal residual (`Transport(raw_goal_theta, Affinity(z_t))`) — i.e., self-affinity modulated an already-computed vector field. This is a fundamentally different use than the literature precedent, which instead adds a **second, additive loss term**: `L_structure = ‖SelfSim(z_t) − SelfSim(z_t^original)‖` (or an analogous query/key self-similarity match), optimized *alongside* — not multiplicatively combined with — the style-goal push. Concretely: keep the native score-residual push exactly as-is (untouched, no reweighting), and add a small independent AdamW loss term that penalizes drift in `z_t`'s own self-similarity/self-attention structure relative to the un-stylized trajectory, exactly analogous to how PnP/Splice-ViT preserve structure as an explicit second signal, not a modulation of the appearance signal.

**(c) Expected effect.** This directly targets DINO layout-leakage (our structure-preservation metric) via a mechanism proven to work for structure preservation in the literature, while leaving the (apparently structurally sound) native residual's style-transport untouched — addressing the likely real bug in our dead carrier: it tried to make one signal do two jobs (transport direction AND structure preservation) via multiplicative reweighting, rather than using two additive, independently-tuned signals.

**(d) Risk.** Adds a second loss and its own weight to tune (another axis of hyperparameter search); if our latent's self-similarity computation is itself noisy or already highly correlated with the native residual's own structure-preserving behavior (i.e., if the residual barely touches layout in the first place, which the carrier-inert result might actually suggest), this could add complexity without moving the Pareto frontier at all — worth a fast eyeball check before a full sweep.

### Idea E — Attention-Distillation-style dual (style + content) self-attention loss folded into the existing AdamW loop (from Attention Distillation, CVPR 2025)

**(a) Mechanism.** Attention Distillation computes an explicit loss between self-attention maps (a "current" vs. an "ideal"/reference-driven stylization) and backpropagates it directly into the noisy latent as an improved classifier-guidance term, paired with a separate content-preserving self-attention loss — i.e., the whole method **is** a test-time latent optimization loop driven by two attention-derived losses, structurally identical in shape to our own AdamW loop over the score residual.

**(b) How it plugs into our method.** Because the shape matches ours almost exactly (loss → gradient → step on `z_t`), the pluggable piece is the *loss definition*, not the optimization mechanism: instead of (or in addition to) our current native score-residual push, add an attention-map-matching term computed directly from FLUX.2's own self-attention maps at the current step (target vs. a reference-denoising branch), optimized inside the same 5 inner AdamW iters we already run.

**(c) Expected effect.** Potentially a strong style-fidelity gain since it operates at the same representational level (attention) the model itself uses to render texture/style, complementary to our score-level residual.

**(d) Risk.** Exact loss formula for the paper could not be verified past the abstract/project-page level in this session (see table note); implementing this without the equation means re-deriving it from the released code (`github.com/xugao97/AttentionDistillation`) rather than the paper text — should be treated as "unverified mechanism, verify via code before committing effort," per this project's own literature-verification norm. Also doubles the forward-pass cost per inner iter (need a second, reference-denoising branch to compare attention maps against), which is nontrivial given the loop already runs at ~5 inner iters × 25 steps.

## 3. Ranked "TRY FIRST" list (top 3, highest leverage / cheapest first)

1. **Idea A — Timestep-scheduled injection strength.** Cheapest by far: no new forward pass, no new loss, just re-parameterize the existing single-scalar carrier/goal-push strength into a cosine or √-shaped schedule over the 25 denoise steps (per Scheduled Style Injection's finding that a fixed global blend constant is provably Pareto-suboptimal vs. a scheduled one, in a directly analogous StyleID setting). Directly tests whether our carrier's "inert at any strength" result was a *magnitude* problem (fixable by scheduling) or a *direction* problem (not fixable by scheduling) — a fast, informative first experiment before investing in anything heavier.

2. **Idea D — Content self-similarity as an additive structure-preservation loss, not a transport-reweighting kernel.** Cheap-to-moderate: reuses the same self-affinity computation our dead carrier already has code for, but changes its role from multiplicative reweighting of the goal residual to an independent additive loss term inside the AdamW loop (following the precedent set by Plug-and-Play / Splicing ViT Features, where self-similarity is always used as a *separate* matched loss, never as a modulation kernel on an unrelated appearance signal). Directly diagnostic of why the carrier died: two jobs (transport + structure) forced onto one signal via multiplication, versus two decoupled signals.

3. **Idea B — RB-Modulation-style Tweedie-projected CSD loss stacked on the native residual.** Moderate cost (extra CSD forward pass per inner iter, needs a working differentiable Tweedie/x0-estimate), but highest expected leverage because it is a genuinely *independent* gradient signal (explicit style-descriptor matching at the estimated clean image) rather than any reweighting of the existing residual — structurally the same test-time-optimization-with-terminal-cost idea used by RB-Modulation (ICLR 2025) and echoed by FlowAlign's terminal-point regularization for rectified flow specifically.

Ideas C (source-side RoPE decoupling) and E (Attention-Distillation dual loss) are noted as higher-leverage-but-higher-risk/verification-needed follow-ups, not first tries: C requires reverse-engineering FLUX.2 internals and a scope call on the latent-only constraint; E's exact loss formula could not be verified from primary sources in this session and needs code-level confirmation before implementation.
